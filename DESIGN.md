# Design

The visual system of the thenwhat site as built. Product context is in `PRODUCT.md`; behavior is in `openspec/`.

**Direction: evidence digest.** Chosen by the operator on 2026-11-11 from three sketches (`.impeccable/mocks/decision/directions.html`), replacing an earlier yellow "timetable" look that did not suit the subject. The brief: calm, familiar to readers of research-summary sites, relevant to the topic, and as simple as the first version.

## Principles

1. **Answer first.** The top of a claim page is a soft panel that says where the claim stands and when the latest record is dated, beside two sentences of summary.
2. **Promise beside record.** What was promised for a year sits beside what was recorded in it. The reader compares; the site does not conclude.
3. **Words carry status.** Status is always written out: wherever a status is shown, its label is in sight. Colour, shape and position only support the words. Decided by the operator on 2026-11-15 (change `record-strip`): what a drawing shows about dates and counts may be said by the drawing alone in sight, provided a key on the page names each mark once and the drawing has a text alternative in sentences. A caption that would repeat on every row moves to that key.
4. **Quiet surface.** One typeface for the page and one for a record page's headings (see Type), one accent colour, one highlight colour. No gradients or photographs. One soft shadow, on popovers only. Motion is listed under Motion and nothing else moves.
5. **Say it once.** A colour or drawing is named once in a key, then used without its words. A record appears once, with its source on the same line; detail opens on demand.

Rules:

- Pale amber means "promised" and nothing else. A promise is always on it, with dashed rules, wherever it appears.
- In the home page's day-by-day list the panel blue is also the ground of what is on record: a row's status column and a registry row's recorded item. White is what was reported (a report's own item is white with a hairline edge); pale amber is what was promised. Every row also says its kind in words. Nothing else takes the panel blue there.
- Inside the extension's popup the popup's own treatment governs, in the extension and where the site draws it on the extension page: the latest promise is a rounded pale amber box (13px corners) with no dashed rule (`extension/src/popup.css`, `.facts .promise`), because the drawn popup must match the real one. Amber still means "promised" and nothing else.
- Amber is kept for a promise that exists. An empty Promised cell is white, with "Nothing promised for this year." for screen readers; the column heading stays amber. Where a past year has a promise and no record, "No record" is in ink at body size, not in grey: the same words, easier to find. A claim with no dated promise has no Promised column, and says "No dated promise on record." above the table.
- There is no colour for any status. No red, no green.
- Two themes, light and dark, with the same meanings. See "Dark theme".

## Layout

- Page width 60rem, gutter `clamp(1rem, 4vw, 2rem)`. Claim and topic pages are laid out in the frame: 78rem wide, a list 13.5rem wide at the left, and the white sheet, on the pale ground `--ground`.
- The home page keeps its day-by-day list's columns all the way down (`--list`, `--rail`, `--column-gap`). From 56rem what is under the list takes the frame's full width. From 72rem each section's heading stands in the left column, under the Browse links, and what it holds spans the sheet and the right column; the lists' heading stays in sight beside them. One hairline across the frame parts each section from the next, with equal space above and below it, as the right column's hairlines part its three parts.
- Corner radius `0.85rem` on panels and table frames, `0.65rem` on the search field and button, full on the topic chip.
- The frame's side columns give way below 68rem: the list becomes one row of chips that stays at the top. This is the only width besides 40rem at which layout changes.
- One breakpoint at 40rem. Below it the digest stacks, and both table types become labelled blocks: each year is a block with a pale amber "Promised for this year" box above "Recorded this year".

## Components

- **Masthead.** The footer's deep navy with white lettering and no hairline, so the page opens and closes on the same colour. The operator asked for it on 2026-11-13, to feel familiar to readers of clinical and health sites. The reverse mark (the operator asked for it on 2026-11-13: no white tile, the glass in white with the navy showing through the lens, the capsule in its own coral and blue), drawn into the page so that its glass and capsule can move (see Motion), then the site name, then three plain links, the first of which is "Look up" (the home page's button says the same); the current one is underlined in the lifted blue. On paper it has no fill, the name is in ink and the mark is printed in one dark colour. With a forced palette the mark keeps a small navy tile of its own. With one listed topic its name is the middle link; with several the link reads "Topics" and leads to the list on the home page. A topic is listed only once it has a published claim.
- **Topic chip.** A pill with a small line drawing for the topic and the topic's name. A topic takes its group's drawing, so dementia is drawn as brain and nerves; one with no drawing gets a plain ring. On hover or focus the drawing is drawn again, line by line: one technique for every topic, and none has a gesture of its own, because an hourglass that turns or a heart that beats would be a joke about a condition. The operator asked on 2026-11-13 for a drawing per group. Twelve are from Lucide: hourglass (ageing), bone, brain, ribbon (cancer), drop (diabetes, weight and hormones), eye, heart with a pulse, shield with a plus (immune system and allergies), microscope (infections), bean (kidneys and bladder), wind (lungs and breathing), baby (pregnancy and fertility). Four are drawn for the site on the same grid, where Lucide has nothing that fits: the tooth, a stomach (gut and liver), a head in profile (mental health) and a face under a fringe (skin and hair). A drawing says what part of the body or kind of illness a topic is about, never how a claim in it stands. It links to the topic page, from the claim page and from the home page's topic list.
- **Wordmark.** The name in sentence case, white on the masthead and ink elsewhere, with a light lead and a heavy tail, closed by a question mark: "What came **of it**?". The question mark is drawn, not typed, so that its dot can be the solid blue marker that also shows where a claim stands; on the masthead the dot is the lifted blue. On a first visit the glass is lowered onto the capsule, the hook is drawn and the dot lands: one sentence, told once. A hover does not tell it again; the capsule is seen closer instead. The operator added the question mark to the name on 2026-11-11, replacing the three waiting dots tried first (`.impeccable/mocks/brand/directions.html`).
- **Follow offer.** Under the digest panel, and again beside the reminders: the Follow pill with one grey sentence beside it. Before: "Lists this claim on the home page, with a note when its status changes. Kept in this browser only: no account, nothing sent." After: "You follow this claim. It is listed on the home page…", the words "the home page" a link to the list. The sentence is the confirmation, in sight. Above it, for a reader who last saw the claim at another status, one semibold line: the tag "Changed", then "When you last looked: X. Now: Y."
- **Keep up.** On the home page under the lists: for a reader who follows nothing, a sentence saying what following and the last-visit day are; then a pill with the feed drawing, "Subscribe to status changes", and a grey line saying it is a feed.
- **Follow.** On a claim page, a pill beside Share: a bookmark and "Follow"; once pressed it stays tinted, the bookmark is filled and it reads "Following". The bookmark lands in the pill that was pressed, on the press only, never when a followed page is opened. It lists the claim on the home page, in this browser only. Shown only where the browser lets the page keep a list.
- **Following (home page).** Above the counts, only for a reader who follows a claim: each claim's headline as the link, then its status in ink, or the tag "Changed" with "Was: X. Now: Y." when the status differs from the one last seen; a changed claim comes first. Each row ends with the text control "Stop following", and the list with "Kept in this browser only." The site's version of the extension's list, in the same words.
- **Since line.** Above the tabs, one line in ink: "Since your last visit on 7 Nov 2026: 12 of 104 news reports, none of 38 status changes." Each count stands beside its list's total, so it is never read against a tab's number. A count is a text control: pressed, it turns to ink, its list shows only the rows dated since, and "Show all" appears at the line's end. None of the status changes is said in plain words; a list with none of the others is left out. A changed followed claim is added: "1 followed claim has changed." A first visit reads "In the last 30 days". Rows dated since the last visit carry the tag "New" after the headline or the name. Counts only.
- **Pills.** Every pill (Share, Follow, the copy and calendar pills, "Narrow these lists") is outlined in `--ring`, 3:1 on white, so its edge can be seen; before 2026-11-14 the outline was the hairline rule.
- **Tag.** "New" and "Changed": 0.72rem bold ink on the tag blue, 0.3rem corners. Always beside words that say what is new or what changed; never alone, never a colour for a status.
- **Text control.** A button that reads as a link: blue, underlined, semibold, no fill. Used for the counts in the since line and for "Stop following".
- **Share.** On a claim page, and under a topic's summary, a quiet pill opposite the topic chip. It opens the device's share sheet, or copies the page address and says "Link copied". Hidden when neither is possible, and on paper.
- **What this is.** Under a claim's headline, one grey line for a reader who arrived from a shared link: "A record of what followed this news report." (or "these news reports"; "this reported breakthrough" when the claim has no article) and the link to the method page.
- **Reports followed.** Straight under that line, before the status: the claim's news reports, newest first and at most three, as a plain list with hairlines. Each is the outlet's title in quotation marks at 1.05rem semibold, linked to the article with the leaving arrow, and under it the outlet and the date in grey. More than three end with a link to the timetable. The sentence above is the list's only visible label; its heading is for screen readers. A reader who pressed a headline on the home page finds that headline here, so the page is seen to be about it.
- **Other claims.** Before "Pass it on": up to three other claims in the same topic, newest report first, in the index table, and a link to the topic when there are more.
- **Another headline?** The last section of a claim page and of a topic page: one sentence and a pill, "Look up another", that leads to the home page's box with the field ready, then the line that suggests a headline where that is configured. A page ends on a next step.
- **Check again later.** Linked from the digest panel, under the dates, once it can be offered. After "Pass it on" on a claim page: a row of the same pills, each with a clock and each saving a calendar file for one date (the promised date when it is still to come, six months, a year, the next anniversary of the first report). The file is made in the browser and nothing is sent. The section is hidden until the script has run, and a choice that cannot be offered is removed. Saving is confirmed in the pill pressed: it is tinted, the clock's long hand turns once, it reads "Saved to your calendar" for two seconds, and a screen reader is told. The clock is drawn for the site on the icon set's grid, so that its hand is a part of its own. Plain wording only: this sits on a claim page.
- **The reader's lists (extension).** Under any answer the popup offers two text controls, "Recently checked" and "Following"; they are a side door and never compete with the status. Each list is a view of its own with "Back", built from the same record cards as a registry search. A followed claim that has moved carries a filled "Changed" tag and the sentence "Was: X. Now: Y.", so the change is never told by the tag alone. Both lists end with "Kept in this browser only." Text controls are blue, underlined and 13px; they are not buttons and take no fill.
- **Unchecked notice.** On a claim page no person has checked: a dashed outline under the digest panel, never above it, so the status is the first thing read.
- **Digest panel.** The status label with an info button, the stage track, the no-signal note in a dashed outline when present, "Promised by <year>" on pale amber with a dashed rule when the claim has a dated promise (the year leads to its row in the timetable), "Latest record <date>", where the date leads to that record in the timetable, and, where the export carries matches, how many registry records were matched and how many are possible, leading to their list, beside what the status means, in one semibold sentence, and the summary under it. For a claim at Trial registered with trials on record, one plain sentence stands between them: how many trials are on record, how many have finished, and that the registry gives none a phase ("3 trials are on record and all have finished. The registry gives none a phase."), so the status is not read as contradicting the trials listed below. It carries no link, because on a phone it is drawn with the meaning, ahead of the path. The status is explained in the open, so no reader has to find the info button to learn what "Preclinical only" says. The info button opens a panel with the records it rests on (each linking to its line in the timetable) and the date the status was set. The no-signal note opens a panel saying what it does and does not mean. The date a status was computed is never presented as the date something happened.
- **Reading order on a phone.** In the digest the sentence that says what the status means comes straight after the status label, ahead of the path and the dates; in "Pass it on" the sentence and its buttons come before the card. Only the drawn order changes, never the order a keyboard moves in.
- **The path, named.** The status label's info panel lists the six places on the path by name, the claim's own in bold with the marker beside it, so the unnamed dots of the track are explained where a reader presses to understand.
- **Stage track.** Under the digest's track one grey line says the place in words ("Place 4 of 6, from preclinical to approved"), the same words a screen reader is given. Six places on a line, from preclinical to approved, with one larger solid blue dot at the status's place. It marks a position, not progress, and is never filled up to the dot. It always sits beside the status in words. Failed, terminated and retracted have no track, except in the day list's status column (openspec `latest-rows`), where a status off the path (no trial record found too) is a 0.65rem blue-edged square alone, no path, read as "Not a place on the path from preclinical to approved". The operator approved it on 2026-11-11 as the one exception to "no meters".
- **The record in brief.** Under the unchecked notice and before the facts, on every claim page: at most six fixed questions about the claim's records, each with a short answer in bold that is read without opening anything. The section's heading, then one grey line: how many answers there are, that opening one shows its record, that they describe the records and not the treatment and are not advice, and a link to the method page. Two groups, "How it was tested" (tested in people; compared with a dummy device or a placebo; what the largest trial measured) and "Where the records stand now" (results posted to the registry; a trial under way; approved by a regulator). A group's name stands in a column of its own beside its rows, as in the topic directory, at 1.1rem bold; the first group opens on a 2px ink rule, the second on a hairline in `--ring`, and the section closes on a 2px ink rule. A question keeps its place whatever its answer, and "None on record" is said where its question sits: no group, heading, mark, colour or order says an answer counts for or against a treatment, and no answer begins with "Yes". With no trial on record the trial questions are left out, "What was the first report about?" takes the second place, and the rest keep their order. Each row is the browser's own disclosure with one chevron that turns when open; it opens without scripting and is printed open. Opened, a pale panel (`--panel`, radius `0.65rem`, at most 62 characters wide) gives one to three plain sentences (the other trials' sizes, the design as the registry states it, a term defined once where it is used: sham, placebo, posted, under way, regulator), then a grey line naming the record as a link and the day it was read ("Read directly from NCT…. Record read 13 Nov 2026."), then a link to the records on the page where there is one. An answer that rests on a model's reading quotes the registry's own words and says "A model read the plain wording from the quoted words, and a rule checked that they are in NCT…. How sure the reading is: very likely · 92%."; with no usable reading the answer is the registry's title in quotation marks and the panel says these are the registry's words. The section carries no posted result value and no p value: those stay in the trial entries, and "See the posted results" stands under the results answer, outside the disclosure. An approval answer names the regulator and the date and says, without being opened, that an approval is not advice to take a treatment. On a phone the group's name is a heading above its rows, the question is a small grey line above its answer, and every row and every link on a line of its own is 44 pixels tall. Hidden from the site's search. Whether a person has checked the page is said by the unchecked notice above and not repeated.
- **At a glance.** From 60rem the terms are two columns of cells, each a label over its value, 2.5rem apart; a cell whose card is open, and an odd last cell, takes the full width (openspec `band-tones`). Below 60rem the layout is as described here. ## Dry wording

The voice is plain, exact and unexcited, and that is the only joke the site makes: its own way of reporting, turned on itself. It is allowed on the site's own furniture and nowhere else.

- Not-found page: "No page record found", the path with no place marked and the glass at rest past it, and "Last checked: just now." Under it, the search and the topics: each by name while there are six or fewer, one link to the directory past that.
- A search with no result: the same drawing, smaller, then "Nothing tracked here matches “…”." and that the site covers only the claims it has tracked so far, with pasting the article's link as the way to check a story (the operator chose to drop "We say that a lot." after the 2026-11-15 search critique).
- Footer: "We will check again." This is a promise; it stays only while the registries are in fact re-checked.
- Page source: one comment for a reader who checks it.

Never on a claim page, a card, an embed or the extension, never about a condition, a treatment, an outlet or the people behind a claim, and never a pun on a topic's name.

## Resilience

- Browser icons include SVG, PNG and a 16/32/48px ICO fallback. Brand asset URLs carry an artwork revision; launcher artwork stays inside the maskable safe area.
- Outlet names and domains wrap as separate groups with a clear gap. A missing or failed publisher image retains its letter tile without shifting the row.

- Nothing widens the page: long names, identifiers and addresses break. Checked at 320 pixels on every page type.
- A hyphenated name stays unbroken only up to 16 characters.
- A popover taller than the screen scrolls inside itself.
- An empty place on the path is outlined in `--ring` (`#7f90a3`, 3:1 on white).
- With a forced palette (Windows high contrast) the marker, the basis mark and the name's dot take the system text colour, and promise areas, the digest and popovers gain an outline.
- `color-scheme: light` keeps form controls and scrollbars light.
- An article with no outlet name is named by its domain.
- Without JavaScript: the home page says search needs it and points to the topic list; popovers still open on tap; the share button stays hidden.
- A slow answer to an earlier search never replaces the answer to a later one.
- The stage marker starts small, never absent, so it is on the page from the first frame.

## Rules for every future page

- Footer notices on every page: no medical advice; a registered trial is not a treatment option; data sources; last-checked date. A build check fails without them.
- No verdict wording in any status label; a build check fails on words such as "abandoned".
- Status never by colour alone.
- Tables carry their table, row and cell roles in the markup, because on a phone they are drawn as blocks and a browser would otherwise stop reporting them as tables.
- Source articles are linked by outlet, title and date. Their text is never shown.
- No stock photography. Topic imagery is a drawn mark in the accent colour.
- Everything pressed is at least 44 pixels tall on a phone: pills, the topic chip, the info button, the no-signal note, record details, the menu and the footer links.
- A link that leaves the site from a record line (a news report's title) carries a small arrow, so a quoted headline that opens the outlet is told from one that opens a claim page.
- A pasted article address never enters the page's own address; only typed search words do.
- A popover panel is a note to a screen reader, and every record line says its kind in words, not by its drawing alone. A mark on a record strip is not a record line: its kind is named in the page's key and the strip as a whole is named in sentences.
- Focus is a 3px blue outline (white on the masthead and the footer). Selection is the tag blue, inverted on the masthead and the footer. The skip link is white with blue lettering, because it lands over the masthead. Hover thickens a link's underline, darkens the chip and the button.
- A date never breaks across two lines.
- Print hides navigation and search, drops the footer's fill and the table frame, never splits a record across sheets, and prints each source address after its link.
