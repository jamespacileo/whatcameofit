# Glossary

Domain terms for thenwhat. Behavior is specified in `openspec/`; this file only fixes the words.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Claim** | One intervention for one indication, as reported in the news. The unit the site tracks. Many articles can report the same claim. |
| **Intervention** | The drug, device or method a claim is about. Stored as the claim's intervention entity, with synonyms for its other names. |
| **Indication** | The condition the intervention is claimed to treat. With the intervention it makes a claim's identity. |
| **Source article** | A news article that reported a claim. Stored as URL, outlet, title and date. Its text is never stored. |
| **Promise** | A timeline stated in a source article ("human trials by 2025"). A claim can have several, each tied to its article. |
| **Record strip** | A small time axis beside a claim with a mark at the date of each news report, registry record, posting of results, regulator decision and dated promise. It ends at the day the registries were last checked. In a list of claims every strip shares one scale; on a claim's page the strip has the claim's own axis. Its marks are named, in these words, in a key: "Registry record", "Results posted", "Regulator decision", "News report", "Promised date", "Registries last checked". |
| **Reading** | Something a model read from a trial's stored registry record into a fixed code, or a number with a unit, kept with the exact words it was read from. A rule checks the words are in the record before a reading is stored. The site words the code; a reading never changes a status, a match score or a trial fact. |
| **Under way** | A trial the registry lists as started and not yet finished. Never "recruiting": the site does not say a trial is open to participants. |
| **The record in brief** | The section of a claim page that answers up to six fixed questions from the claim's records, each answer opening to the record it was read from. |
| **Reported statement** | Something a source article says about a claim's intervention beyond its timeline, such as an effect it reports or a hope it has a researcher voice. Written in the site's own words, never a quotation, and naming no one. Recorded with its kind, how firmly the article puts it (as fact or as a possibility), who the article has saying it (a role), and what the article says it rests on. Checked against its article, with the probability that the article says it as its confidence, and approved by rule when that is high. Not a claim, which is one intervention for one indication, and not a promise, which is a timeline. What a report said, not what is on record, and never an input to status. |
| **Vertical** | The topic area a claim belongs to, for example gum disease. Each claim has exactly one. |
| **Group** | The body system or disease area a vertical is filed under, for example mouth and teeth. Every vertical has exactly one group. Each group is anchored, in the pipeline's configuration only, to the HRCS health categories it covers. |
| **General vertical** | The vertical that shares its group's slug and title. A claim that fits a group but none of its specific verticals goes here. |
| **Approach** | The kind of intervention a claim reports, such as drug, gene therapy or transplant. Optional, at most one per claim, and never part of status. |
| **Entity** | Something a claim names: an intervention, paper, person, institution or company. Has a name and synonyms. |
| **Organisation** | An institution or company a claim names: one a source article names, or the sponsor of one of its trials. Identified when a match to a record in an open organisation registry is approved, and then shown with that record's name, kinds, country and website. Listed once per identity, with every role it plays for the claim. A person is never an organisation. Who an organisation is is stated, never judged, and it is never an input to status. |
| **Sponsor** | The organisation a trial's registry record names as its lead sponsor. A sponsor that is a person is not recorded and not shown. |
| **Search phrase** | Words a registry or a paper would use for an intervention that has no product name, such as "median nerve stimulation". Searched for within the claim's indication only. Not a name, and never shown as one. |
| **Development code** | A code a substance registry lists for a drug, such as CIN-107 for baxdrostat. Taken only from the one substance the registry lists under the intervention's names, and kept with that substance's record. Searched for like a search phrase, within the claim's indication only. Not a name, and never shown as one. |
| **Origin paper** | The publication the first news reports of a claim were about. At most one per claim. |
| **Related paper** | A published paper about a claim's intervention and indication that the literature search found and the article did not cite. Stored as a match on the intervention, with a score and the reasons found by rule. Approved by rule when it scores high. It is not an evidence event and never changes the status. |
| **Match** | A proposed link between an entity and a record in a public registry (a trial, a DOI). Approved by rule when its score is in the high band; a person can reject it afterwards. |
| **Subject term** | An entry in an open vocabulary, such as a MeSH heading, proposed for a claim's condition or its intervention, or a human gene proposed as its target. A candidate is gathered by rule: the registry files the claim's approved trials under it, its label or an entry term of it equals one of the claim's names, or a word of the intervention's names equals a gene's symbol or a listed synonym of it (a named gene). Each candidate is scored for the claim, read as exact or broader, and approved or rejected by rule; the operator can reject an approved one. A kind link ties the intervention to the term of its intervention kind and is scored and decided the same way. A drug link ties the intervention to a drug source's record of it, found by the drug's own name being equal to one of the intervention's names; it has no score. Never an input to status. |
| **Entry term** | Another wording a vocabulary files under one of its terms, such as "Ketogenic Diet" under the MeSH heading "Diet, Ketogenic". A name equal to an entry term makes the term a candidate, as a name equal to its label does. A wording that only contains the name does not. |
| **Intervention kind** | What sort of treatment an intervention is, chosen from a fixed list held in the project (drug, antibody, device, surgical procedure and so on), each tied to one vocabulary term. Chosen by a decision only for an intervention with no approved exact subject term, never taken from the registry's type. |
| **Background card** | What the site shows about a subject term: its label, its definition in the vocabulary publisher's own words, the source and the vocabulary's version, the link's score and reasons, and the date the record was read. A card from a kind link is for the intervention kind and says so. A card from a named gene describes the gene's product in its source's words and states no mechanism. A drug card gives the kind of molecule and what the drug's record says it acts on, never how far the drug has got or what it is used for. A card describes a term. It is not evidence for a claim and not advice. |
| **Evidence event** | A dated, sourced fact about a claim, such as a trial being registered or a follow-up paper appearing. Status comes from approved evidence events; the evidence stage matters only when no trial is found. |
| **Follow-up paper** | A paper that cites the origin paper and either shares an author with it or names the intervention. |
| **Review state** | Pending, approved or rejected. Applies to draft claims, matches and evidence events. A decision is made by a rule, a human, the seed loader or an assistant; approval by rule publishes. |
| **Archived** | Taken off the public site by the operator. The address answers 410; the database keeps the record. Not a review state. |
| **Confidence** | A match's score shown as a word and a percentage, such as "High match · 92%", with its reasons. An automatic estimate of matching the reported treatment and condition, never a verdict on the treatment. |
| **Source** | An article or post the pipeline read: its address, outlet, title and date, never its text. Has a page listing its claim or up to five candidates. |
| **Stage reading** | How a model read a claim's evidence stage from its first report, with a score. In the sure band it confirms the stage by rule. In the lower band it is shown on the claim page marked low confidence, and decides nothing. |
| **Status** | Where a claim stands, derived by fixed rules from its approved evidence events: preclinical only, no trial record found, trial registered, Phase 1 to 3, approved, failed, terminated or retracted. With no trial found, the confirmed evidence stage decides between the first two: a study in people or an unknown stage is "no trial record found"; confirmed laboratory or animal work is "preclinical only". |
| **Standing trial** | A trial that is neither terminated nor withdrawn and carries no failure flag. Status comes from the furthest standing trial that has started. |
| **Trial facts** | What a trial's registry record states of the trial itself: participants, design, sponsor, planned primary outcomes and posted results. Read by fixed rules from the record stored with the trial's evidence events, shown with the date the record was read, and never an input to status. |
| **Results paper** | A paper that a trial's registry record names: listed by the sponsor as reporting the study's results, or linked by the registry from PubMed. Attached to the trial by rule, with no search, score or model. Papers the record lists as background are not results papers. |
| **Paper facts** | What a paper's index record states of the paper itself: title, journal, year, kinds of publication, open access, retracted, and whether its subject headings say the study was in people, in animals or both. Read by fixed rules from the stored Europe PMC record, shown with the date the record was read, and never an input to status. |
| **Failure flag** | A human-set marker that a trial missed its primary endpoint, with a source link. The only way a claim becomes "failed". |
| **Gloss** | A status in plain words, for a reader who does not know trial phases: one fixed phrase per status, worded by the site. It claims no more than the status rule knows and is never a status itself. |
| **Overlay** | Extra text on a status, not a status itself. The no-signal overlay says how many years have passed without a new evidence event. |
| **Decision step** | A bounded classification made through the `decide` interface, such as scoring a match. Always advisory. |
| **Human label** | The operator's review decision recorded against a decision step's suggestion. Used later to evaluate and calibrate. |
| **Export** | The JSON file of approved data that the site is built from. |
| **Seed** | A checked-in claim loaded as already reviewed so a fresh checkout has a page. Its decisions are marked as the seed loader's, not an operator's. |
| **Operator** | The person who runs the pipeline, corrects decisions and archives wrong items. |
| **Step** | One kind of processing applied to one kind of record in a backfill, such as the news search for a registry trial. It has a name and a version, and declares which records it applies to and which source it asks. |
| **Work ledger** | The record of which step has been completed for which record at which version. A run processes only what the ledger does not show as finished. |
| **Due** | A record is due for a step when it is in the step's scope and the ledger has no finished entry for it at the step's current version, or the fields the step reads have changed since. |
| **Allowance** | How many requests a source may receive in a period and how far apart, shared by every process that asks that source. |
| **Registry trial** | A trial as a registry lists it, loaded in bulk and kept whether or not any claim refers to it. Not an evidence event and never exported. |
| **Article lead** | A news article that a search found for a registry trial, stored as address, outlet, title and date. Unreviewed and never published. Not a source article: it belongs to no claim until a later step makes it one. |
| **Outlet kind** | Whether an article lead's or a link lead's outlet is general news, health or clinical trade press, a press release wire, or on no list. Set by rule from the outlet's domain. |
| **Link lead** | The address of an article that people shared on a social platform, loaded from a bulk file and kept once per address, with each share's platform, community, time and score and the title it was shared under. Never who shared it. Unreviewed and never published. Not an article lead, which a search found for a registry trial, and not a source article: it belongs to no claim until its article is read. |
| **Screening** | The rule checks on a link lead that use stored data only: whether its outlet is listed, whether its address is already a source article, and which claims and registry trials the database already holds for it. No request and no model. |
| **Triage** | The decision step that scores, from a link lead's title alone, whether the article reports a specific treatment for a specific condition. The score passes, holds or rejects the lead by two thresholds the operator sets; it creates and changes nothing. |
| **Topic** | A subject the operator wants covered first, such as GLP-1 drugs: a name, and the words that mark a link lead's title or a claim's intervention as belonging to it. A lead that names one is read before the others, and is read when triage held it. A topic changes only what is read and in what order: it is never a status, never a vertical, and never in the export. |
| **Lead label** | What a link lead's headline is, as a person or an assistant says: the answer to the question triage scores. It belongs to the lead, so it holds for every later version of triage. An assistant's label is recorded as an assistant's, never as a person's, and counts only where no person has labelled the lead. The operator spot-checks an assistant's labels. |
