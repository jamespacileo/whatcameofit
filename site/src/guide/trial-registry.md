## In the records

The World Health Organization sets a minimum of 24 items a register must show for a trial to count as fully registered. They include the trial's identifying number, the date of registration, its recruitment status and its primary outcome.

Medical journal editors, through the International Committee of Medical Journal Editors, recommend that a trial be registered at or before the first patient's consent, as a condition of being considered for publication. The WHO gathers the records of a network of primary registries; it is not itself a registry. ClinicalTrials.gov is run by the United States National Library of Medicine.

## What it does not tell you

A record is the sponsor's own account. ClinicalTrials.gov says the sponsor or investigator submits the information and is responsible for its accuracy, and that the United States government does not review or approve the safety and science of all the studies listed.

The rules on what must be registered, and when, differ between bodies and countries. A study missing from one registry is therefore not shown never to have happened.

A record can be out of date. It changes only when its sponsor updates it.
