## In the records

In the United States, the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) says approval of a drug means that its reviewers have examined the data on the drug's effects and determined that its benefits outweigh its known risks for the intended population. The FDA does not test drugs itself; it reviews the evidence a company sends.

In the European Union the decision is called a marketing authorisation. The European Medicines Agency gives a scientific recommendation and the European Commission takes the binding decision. In the United Kingdom the Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency grants a marketing authorisation, also called a licence.

Some authorisations are conditional: granted on less comprehensive data than normally required, for serious conditions, and renewed each year.

## What it does not tell you

An approval is for a stated use. It does not cover every condition a treatment has been reported for.

Each regulator decides for its own territory, so a decision by one is not a decision by another.

Authorised does not mean available or paid for. The European Medicines Agency says pricing and reimbursement are decided nationally and that it has no role in them.

A conditional authorisation rests on evidence that is still being completed.
