## In the records

The Committee on Publication Ethics describes retraction as a way of correcting the literature and alerting readers to articles whose content or data are so seriously flawed that the findings cannot be relied on. It says the purpose is to correct the literature, not to punish.

The medical journal editors' committee says a retraction should be prominently labelled, appear on a page that is indexed, and carry the original article's title in its heading. The United States National Library of Medicine links each retraction notice to the article's citation in PubMed. Crossref publishes the Retraction Watch database of retractions, gathered from publishers' websites.

## What it does not tell you

A retraction does not by itself say there was misconduct. The Committee on Publication Ethics lists honest error and naive mistakes beside misconduct as causes, and the National Library of Medicine does not distinguish between them in PubMed. The notice is where the journal gives its reason.

It concerns one paper. It does not say other research on the same treatment is unsound.

No list of retractions is complete. Crossref says many retractions in the database had not been reported by its own member publishers.

A correction and an expression of concern are different notices, and neither is a retraction.
