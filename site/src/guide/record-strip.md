## In the records

The line runs from left to right, oldest to newest, and ends at a heavier upright: the day the registries were last checked. A short notch under the line falls every five years.

Each mark stands for one dated thing. A short line rising from the axis is a registry record, such as a trial being registered or its state changing. A small square above the axis is a trial posting its results. A diamond on the axis is a regulator's decision. A hollow ring on the axis is a news report. A dashed amber flag is a date a report promised, drawn only when the report gave a date that can be placed.

The marks come from the same dated records the claim page lists, so every mark can be found there with its source. Beside the strip on the home page are counts in numerals: how many trials are on record and how many have posted results.

## What it does not tell you

It does not say what a record found. A square says results were posted on that date, and nothing about what they were. The claim page links to each record.

An empty stretch of line is time in which no record is dated. It does not say the work has stopped: research that is not yet registered or published leaves no mark.

It does not say whether a promised date was met. The flag shows where the date falls; any record near it is there to be compared.

It does not show size. A trial of forty people and a trial of four thousand have the same mark, and many marks close together are many records, not stronger evidence.

A promise with no date, or a date after the last check, has no mark at all.
