## In the records

Publishing databases record which papers cite which. This site looks up the papers that cite a claim's origin paper and keeps those that are likely to continue the same work: a paper with an author in common, or one that names the treatment.

Each one kept is shown on the claim's timeline with the date it was published and a link to its entry in the database. Its summary is one line written by this site; the paper's text is not copied.

## What it does not tell you

It does not say the later paper confirmed the first. A paper that cites another may support it, qualify it, fail to repeat it or only mention it. This site records that the paper exists and when, and does not read its findings.

It does not say that no other research followed. Work by other groups that does not cite the origin paper, or does not name the treatment, is not found by this rule.

A follow-up paper counts as a new record, so it resets the count behind a no-signal note. That says something was published, and nothing about what it showed.
