## In the records

Each claim carries a mark saying whether a person has reviewed it. A page put together by software, from a seed file or by an assistant's decision, carries a notice under its status until the operator has read it against its sources.

The same holds for this guide. Each entry is drafted from the sources it lists and says so until it has been read.

One status always involves a person. “Trial missed its primary endpoint” is set only when a person has marked the trial, with a link to the source.

## What it does not tell you

“Checked” means a person compared the page with the records it links to. It does not mean anyone assessed the treatment, repeated the research or contacted the researchers.

It does not make a status an opinion. Checked or not, the status is derived by the same fixed rules from the same records; a person can remove a wrong match, and the rules then produce the status again.

A page that has not been checked is not known to be wrong. Its matches carry their confidence scores so that a reader can weigh them, and every record links to its source.
