## In the records

The World Health Organization defines a clinical trial, for registration, as any research study that assigns people in advance to one or more health-related interventions in order to evaluate the effects on health outcomes. The interventions it names include drugs, cells and other biological products, surgical procedures, devices and behavioural treatments.

ClinicalTrials.gov uses “clinical trial” as another name for an interventional study: researchers assign participants to an intervention, sometimes by chance. It lists observational studies too, in which people are assessed and the researcher assigns nothing.

Each study has one registry record with an identifier. That record is what a claim page links.

## What it does not tell you

That a trial exists does not say the treatment works. A trial is the means of finding out, and ClinicalTrials.gov notes that the intervention being tested is often not approved for doctors to prescribe.

The word does not mean a drug study only, and the definitions do not require a comparison group.

A record on a registry is not always a trial in this sense. Some are observational studies.
