## In the records

ClinicalTrials.gov describes a clinical trial as research in which participants are assigned to an intervention to test what happens in people. It says the intervention is often investigational, meaning not approved for doctors to prescribe, and that participants may not receive the intervention being tested: some are assigned the standard treatment or none.

The United States Food and Drug Administration says an investigational drug is still being tested to determine whether it can be used to treat a condition, and that such a product may, or may not, be effective. It adds that trials have strict criteria for who can be included.

A registry record also carries a recruitment status. Many records are for studies that have not begun recruiting, are no longer recruiting, or have ended.

## What it does not tell you

A registry record does not say that the trial is open, or that any particular person could take part.

It does not say the treatment helps. That is the question the trial was set up to answer.

A listing is not an endorsement. ClinicalTrials.gov says the United States government does not review or approve the safety and science of all the studies it lists.

It does not describe access outside a trial. Regulators handle that through separate routes, which a trial record does not cover.
