## In the records

A claim joins two things: the treatment (a drug, a device or a method) and the condition it was reported to treat. Many news reports can describe the same claim, so a claim page lists every report this site has read for it, each with its outlet, title and date and a link to the article. The article's text is never stored or shown.

The same treatment reported for a different condition is a different claim, with its own page, records and status.

## What it does not tell you

“Claim” is used here in its plain sense: something that was reported. It does not mean the report was exaggerated or mistaken, and it does not mean it was correct.

A claim is not an article. A status belongs to the treatment and the condition, and says nothing about the quality of any one report or outlet.

A claim being tracked does not mark it as important or promising. Claims are added because they were reported, and anyone can suggest one.

A claim's headline on this site is a short description written for its page. The outlets' own titles are shown beside it, in quotation marks.
