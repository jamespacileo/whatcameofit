## In the records

ClinicalTrials.gov defines the primary outcome measure as the planned outcome measure, in the study's protocol, that is the most important for evaluating the effect of an intervention. Each one is registered with a title, a description and a time frame. Measures of lesser importance are registered as secondary outcome measures.

The international guideline on trial statistics, ICH E9, treats “primary variable” and “primary endpoint” as the same thing, says there should generally be only one, and says it should generally be the one used to work out how many people the trial needs. The World Health Organization lists the primary outcome among the items every registered trial must show.

## What it does not tell you

A trial that did not meet its primary endpoint has not been shown to have found nothing. Its other measures, and what it recorded on safety, are separate.

A trial that met its primary endpoint has not thereby been approved. A regulator reviews the evidence as a whole.

The words differ between bodies and mean the same: primary outcome measure, primary outcome, primary endpoint. ClinicalTrials.gov notes that some studies have more than one.
