Directions for the front page

The lookup stays the first thing on the page in every mock. What changes is everything under it: how a headline and its record are shown together, how much of the answer is on the page before any press, and what gives a reader a reason to open the page again on Thursday.

Each mock is a working page built from the published export of 13 Nov 2026: 84 claims, 104 news reports. Sorting, filters, “show more” and the lookup work. The only made-up value is the reader’s last visit (7 Nov). Scroll the phones here, or open a page at full size.

What makes a list worth scrolling

On Reddit the title opens a question and the row closes it without a press: a score, a flair, a top comment. The outcome differs from row to row, so the next one is worth a look. Here the quoted headline is the question, and the record is the answer.

What a row can teach, from records we hold

  • How long the idea has been in trials. “Scientists discover £20-a-month plant pill”: first trial registered in 2012, 14 years before the report.
  • What the report was of. An animal study, laboratory work, an early or late human study.
  • What the registries hold. 5 trials on record, 3 completed, 2 with results posted.

What brings a reader back

  • New headlines. 9 reports in the last seven days, about 40 in thirty.
  • Records that move. 11 registry records dated in the last 45 days, 2 approvals since September.
  • Dates that arrive. 10 promises name a date; one falls due on 31 Dec 2026.

Second round: two mixes of “Then and now” and “Today’s edition”

Both keep the stream’s row (the small digest panel under each headline) and the edition’s dated head with its seven-day strip. They differ in where the edition’s other parts go: above the stream as a briefing, or into the stream as rows of their own.

First round: six directions

Every mock keeps DESIGN.md as it stands: the navy masthead, the pale blue band, one face, pale amber for a promise only, no colour for a status, the outlet’s title in quotation marks, the stage track as a position and never a meter.
