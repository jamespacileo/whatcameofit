# Proposal

## Why

On 2026-11-17 the claim page was critiqued on a phone beside examine.com (`.impeccable/critique/`). Three findings stand on `main` after that day's other fixes, and the operator asked for all of them to be fixed:

- A claim reported as approved ("EU approves erenumab…") shows "Phase 3 trial" in its status card. The record in brief already says the site reads no regulator's records, but a screen and a half below the status. On the first screen the page reads as wrong.
- Who put the page together and when its records were last read are said far from the title: the "not yet checked by a person" notice in a box under the status card, the last-checked day in the footer. The operator chose to say both in one line under the title, where a reader looks for a byline.
- The "Why this status" panel lists every record the status rests on. For erenumab that is 44 links in a panel 400 pixels tall, with no control that closes it.

## What Changes

- Under a "Phase 3 trial" status with no approval listed, the status card says, after what the status means: "No approval is listed here. This site does not read regulators' records, so one can exist and not be listed." It is decided by the status code and the claim's records, never by a headline's wording.
- A line under the title says, for a claim no person checked, "Put together by software, not yet checked by a person", with the last words leading to the guide entry, and then "Registries last checked" with the day. The dashed notice under the status card is removed: the page says it once.
- The "Why this status" panel names the three latest records the status rests on and, when there are more, says how many there are and leads to the timetable, which marks them all. The panel and the no-signal panel end with a "Close" control that stays at the panel's foot.
- On a phone, the two links under the title are as tall as a fingertip.

## Not changing

- No sentence says what a news report claimed. The export carries no such code, and reading it from a title would be a guess.
- The masthead stays two rows on a phone. One row needs the links behind a menu, which hides the way to the lookup.
- The lookup's and the extension's unchecked note keep their wording.
