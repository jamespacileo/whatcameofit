# Proposal

## Why

A claim page's title was the claim's headline, set large in the site's own voice: "EU approves erenumab to prevent migraine…" above a status card reading "Phase 3 trial". The phone critique of 2026-11-17 and its re-run both named it the page's first problem: the first screen gives two answers to the reader's one question. The site's rule is that it never repeats a headline's wording as its own, and that the unit is the claim, one intervention for one indication. On 2026-11-17 the operator chose to lead the page with the claim.

## What Changes

- The title of a claim page is the claim: the intervention's name in the heading face and, under it in the text face and a quieter ink, the indication. Both are drawn as the record gives them; a screen reader hears "for" between them.
- The claim's headline is no longer drawn on the claim page. What was reported stays where it is quoted with its outlet and date: under the title on a wide screen, at the head of the sheet on a phone.
- On the erenumab page the status moves 29 pixels up a phone's first screen.

## Not changing

- Lists, search results, the link-preview card, the embed, the feeds and the open data keep the headline: a list row already leads with the intervention's name and sets the headline under it, quieter. The card and the embed set the headline beside the status and are worth the same look; that is a later change.
- The browser tab's title, which was already the intervention and the indication.
- The export. No field is added or read differently.
