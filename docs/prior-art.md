# Prior art

Existing sites that cover part of what thenwhat does. Checked on 2026-11-12 with web searches. "Unconfirmed" means the search found nothing current either way.

thenwhat's unit is a claim: one intervention for one indication, as reported in the news, linked to registered trials and given a reviewed status. None of the sites below works at that level.

## Checking health news

| Site | What it does | Status on 2026-11-12 | Gap against thenwhat |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| HealthNewsReview.org (Gary Schwitzer) | Graded health news stories against ten criteria. | Reviews ended December 2018 when the grant expired. The site was taken down in late 2022. Schwitzer still writes a Substack, with posts dated 2026. | Judged a story on the day it ran. Never followed a claim afterwards. |
| NHS "Behind the Headlines" (nhs.uk/news) | Evidence analysis of the day's health stories, produced by Bazian for NHS Choices from 2007. | Unconfirmed. The latest mention found is from 2020. | Same day only, no follow-up. |
| Science Media Centre | Expert reaction to stories as they break. | Active. | Aimed at journalists. No tracking over time. |
| STAT trackers (CRISPR, Breakthrough Devices) | Follow one technology or one FDA programme. | Active. | Grouped by topic, not by news claim. Partly paywalled. |

## Trial transparency

| Site | What it does | Status on 2026-11-12 | Gap against thenwhat |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FDAAA TrialsTracker (Bennett Institute, Oxford) | Flags US trials that are overdue to report results, by sponsor. | Paused from February 2024, then resumed in March 2025. Says it updates every working day. | Groups trials by sponsor compliance, not by claim. Not written for the public. |
| EU TrialsTracker | The same for the EU register (EUCTR). | Updates monthly. At risk once CTIS fully replaces EUCTR. | As above. |
| OpenTrials (Goldacre, Open Knowledge) | Linked registry entries, papers and results for each trial. | Phase I ended around 2017. No later activity found. | Linked records for each trial. Had no news layer and gave no status. |

## Finding a trial to join

| Site | What it does | Status on 2026-11-12 | Gap against thenwhat |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Be Part of Research (NIHR, UK) | Plain-language search and matching for UK studies, drawn from ClinicalTrials.gov and ISRCTN. | Active, funded by DHSC. | Recruits for trials that are still running. Says nothing about what happened to a claim. thenwhat must never look like this (no medical advice). |
| Antidote (formerly TrialReach) | Patient-to-trial matching built into patient communities. | Active (partnership news from 2026). | Paid for by sponsors to recruit patients. |
| ClinicalTrials.gov, ISRCTN | The official registries. | Active. | They hold the raw data thenwhat reads. Not written for the public. |
| Leal Health, TrialX, CenterWatch | Patient matching and trial listings. | Unconfirmed in this check. | Recruitment, as above. |

## Evidence summaries and pipeline trackers

Listed from memory and not checked here: Cochrane plain-language summaries (one per review, slow), Examine.com (supplements only), Consensus and Elicit (AI answers from papers, no status, no review step), and BioPharmCatalyst, Citeline and Evaluate (drug pipelines for investors, mostly paywalled).

## Evidence that the problem exists

Dumas-Mallet et al. (PLOS ONE, 2017), as reported by [NPR](https://www.npr.org/sections/health-shots/2017/03/06/518802242/reports-of-medical-breakthroughs-often-dont-prove-out): about half of the medical findings covered in newspapers did not hold up in later studies, and newspapers rarely reported those follow-up studies. That gap is what thenwhat fills.
